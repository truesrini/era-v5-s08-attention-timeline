Attention, in the order it happened

Vanilla attention was not wrong. It was expensive. It sends two bills, compute that grows as T² and a KV cache that grows with every token of every conversation, and it needs a third thing, position, that it cannot provide itself. Every mechanism below is somebody looking at one of those bills at a particular moment and trying to pay less of it. They are laid out by the date each one actually appeared, with the source for that date one click away.

1 · Standard attention, computed in front of you

Six tokens, four numbers each. Every value below is computed live in your browser from fixed toy weights. Step through the pipeline Q·Kᵀ → scale → mask → softmax → weighted sum of V, then switch off the causal mask and watch weight leak onto tokens that have not happened yet.

Two facts carry the rest of the page. Every query is compared with every key, so the score grid is T×T. And nothing in the arithmetic knows token order: identical tokens at positions 2 and 20 produce identical keys. One bill and one missing feature.

2 · The two bills, and what each mechanism cuts

The KV cache for one conversation is 2 × layers × kv_heads × head_dim × T × bytes. Most of the timeline is the field attacking these factors one at a time. The yardstick is the class's: 48 layers, head dim 128, bf16. Rows other than MHA, GQA and MQA are illustrative re-scalings on that yardstick, not the real models.

3 · The timeline at a glance

Each dot is a mechanism, placed on its launch date and in the lane of the bill it mainly attacks. Background bands are what the field wanted most at the time. Dashed arcs run from the day an idea was published to the day a major model shipped it. Click a dot to jump to its card.

Taught order versus launch order

The class built the ideas in teaching order: easiest arithmetic first. Put the same topics on the calendar and the lines cross. Linear attention and the delta rule were taught second and third, yet they are 2020-2021 ideas that only shipped in 2025; DroPE was taught sixth and is almost the newest thing here.
